# Content strategy: what else to build

Two questions this answers. What does the reference catalog actually cover, and
what should a Christian-worldview library look like — which is not the same
thing as the same list with verses bolted on.

> Sourcing note: the Self-Talk Plus / Self-Talk Store sites could not be loaded
> directly from this environment, so the catalog below is assembled from search
> results and may be incomplete. Treat it as a map of the territory, not an
> inventory.

## 1. What the reference catalog sells

The library is advertised at 125+ sessions. The program families that surface
consistently:

**Core "Positive Self-Talk for ___" series**

- Self-Esteem
- Personal Growth / Personal Development
- Taking Control of Your Life
- Positive Relationships
- Health & Fitness
- Career Achievement
- Financial Success
- Quality of Life
- Mastering Goals
- Mastering Time
- Selling

**Standalone / book-linked**

- Stress, Anxiety and Depression
- Weight Loss

**Structure.** Big topics are not one deep track — they are a suite of four
(Morning / Daytime / Daytime / Night), and the largest get progressive six-session
suites that move core beliefs → planning → execution → pressure → review →
mastery. The unit of sale is the suite, not the track.

You already hold four of these: Personal Development, Positive Relationships,
Taking Control of Your Life, and Mastering Time.

## 2. The problem with a straight port

This is the part worth reading twice, because it decides whether the scripts
hold together.

The reference material rests on a total internal locus of control. Four
load-bearing claims:

- "What you make of yourself is up to you and no one else"
- "You never rely on others to create your successes"
- "Your potential is as unlimited as the pictures you create in your mind"
- "You alone are responsible for your own personal growth"

Ported unchanged into a Christian frame, those sit directly against "apart from
me you can do nothing" (John 15:5) and "it is God who works in you" (Phil 2:13).
A script that says both is not deep, it is incoherent — and the listener will
feel the seam even if they cannot name it.

**The resolution is not to soften the agency.** Scripture is relentlessly
imperative: walk, put off, put on, stand, take up, be strong. What changes is
where the power comes from, not how much is asked of you.

Philippians 2:12–13 is the hinge for this entire project:

> work out your own salvation with fear and trembling, **for** it is God who
> works in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure.

Full effort, borrowed source. Paul says it again flatly in 1 Corinthians 15:10:
"I worked harder than any of them — though it was not I, but the grace of God
that is with me."

So the sentence pattern shifts:

| Reference form | Adapted form |
|---|---|
| "You choose X. It is up to you." | "You choose X, **because** you have been given Y." |
| "You are as unlimited as the pictures you create in your mind." | "You are what God has declared you to be." |
| "You never rely on others." | "You do your part, and you never carry it alone." |
| "You never fear failure." | "When you fail, you confess, and you are forgiven." |

Note what the right-hand column buys you. The declarative present-tense
technique wants claims that are **true regardless of how the listener feels**
that morning — and indicative identity statements ("you are chosen, adopted,
sealed, redeemed") are exactly that, in a way "you are an achiever" never is.
The theology makes the technique work better, not worse.

**Two genre gaps the adaptation should fill rather than inherit:**

- **Failure.** The reference format has no category for it — only "you never
  fear failure," which is denial, not an answer. Confession and grace are a
  better one, and there is no analog to port. Write it.
- **Suffering.** The genre's blanket optimism has nothing to say to real grief.
  The Psalms do. A lament track is a genuine addition, not a softening.

### The method is already in the Bible

Worth stating plainly, because it changes how you think about this whole
project. Second-person self-address is a biblical form:

- **Psalm 42:5** — "Why are you cast down, O my soul? ... **Hope in God**."
- **Psalm 103:1** — "Bless the LORD, O my soul."
- **Psalm 62:5** — "For God alone, O my soul, wait in silence."

Martyn Lloyd-Jones' reading of Psalm 42 is the classic articulation: the
psalmist's problem is that he has been *listening* to himself instead of
*talking* to himself, and the cure is to "take yourself in hand, address
yourself, preach to yourself, question yourself."

You are not importing a secular technique and baptizing it. You are using a
modern production format for something the Psalter already does.

## 5. What to build next

Not all of it. Three suites, in this order — and only after the Ephesians suite
is finished and listened to (see `ROADMAP.md`, M1 and M2).

**1. Identity in Christ (Ephesians 1)** — highest leverage. Every other suite
assumes the indicative this one establishes; "you choose to walk worthy" is
groundless until "you are chosen, adopted, redeemed, sealed" is settled. It is
also the easiest to write, because Ephesians 1:3–14 is already a list of
declarative present-tense identity statements. It wants almost no adaptation.

**2. Peace & Anxiety (Philippians 4)** — highest felt need, and the suite that
most needs a *night* track, which the library does not have a single example of
yet. Building it forces you to solve the night format once.

**3. Psalms of Self-Address** — the smallest and the most validating. Mostly
near-verbatim scripture in a form that already matches the technique. Good
proof that the method is native rather than borrowed.

Then reassess. Twelve suites at four tracks each is 48 tracks; at three finished
tracks you will know far more about what you actually listen to than any plan
written now can predict.

## 6. Suite sizing

Follow the reference structure, adapted:

```
01_Morning     second person, continuous       identity and direction
02_Daytime     first person x3 + coda          the foundational content
03_Daytime     first person x3 + coda          the tactical content
04_Night       second person, slow             release, trust, rest
```

Not every suite needs four. **Identity** and **Repentance** may only ever want a
morning and a night. **Lament**, **Sabbath**, and **Waiting** are night-first and
may never need a daytime drill at all. Let the topic decide; the pipeline does
not care.
